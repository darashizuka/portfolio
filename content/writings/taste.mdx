Taste is the shiny new *"skill"* all the tech bros in Silicon Valley are obsessing over right now. Naturally, I fell down the rabbit hole reading about what it is, how to cultivate it, and how to build that muscle. Turns out, it *isn't* new at all. It has always been here; people just used to call it having a good instinct or a good eye. What *has* changed is **the stakes**.

Before sitting down to write this, I actually asked AI to generate an essay on taste for me. Unsurprisingly, it spat out something that read like a *sanitized summary from the internet*. Ironically, that blandness is the exact reason I decided to write this *(haven't written anything original since 12th grade English Literature)*.

---

For years, software engineers were mocked for lacking creativity, which is practically why UI/UX designers had to exist in the first place. Now, suddenly, everyone in tech wants to talk about taste. But taste is more than just creativity or making things look pretty. **It is conviction.** It is the ability to discern *what actually holds value*, *what should be ruthlessly discarded*, and *what outpaces cheap imitation*.

Of course, nobody is born with it. You build taste through **deliberate exposure**, literally wiring new neural pathways. To do that, you have to feed your brain things that have nothing to do with your day job or work. In machine learning terms, it’s basically **transfer learning**: you step outside your bubble, absorb and explore different things for example, *architecture, fashion, literature, or film* and **cross-pollinate** those references back into your own work.

Still, *passive scrolling isn't enough*. You have to study great work, interrogate *why* certain choices were made, and make plenty of bad calls of your own until your gut sharpens into conviction. None of it happens by accident; it takes **intentional effort**. If you want a real litmus test for whether you actually have taste, I think you can check two things:

1. Can you articulate ***why*** you like something rather than just pointing at ***what*** you like?
2. And more importantly, **do people come to you asking for your opinions?**

---

Here is the irony, though: *isn't taste just pattern recognition at scale?* If it is, shouldn't AI eventually beat us at it? 

Maybe at spotting patterns but **conviction doesn't scale, does it?** 

AI doesn't know how to *break a pattern on purpose*. It cannot look at an established rulebook, get sick of it, and invent the next cultural wave, because real cultural shifts stem from **boredom and lived friction**. By design, AI leans toward clichés. It predicts whatever is *most typical, most agreeable, and carries the highest statistical probability*.

> Models will always give us the safest average of what has already worked. **Whereas taste is having the nerve to bet on what hasn't.**
